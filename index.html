Four timbers we keep in the rack

All air-dried at the shop for at least two years, then kiln-finished to 9% before anything is cut.

Teak

Tectona grandis

Oily enough to shrug off water and termites, which is why it has held up in Gujarati houses for two hundred years. It planes cleanly, moves very little between June and March, and takes an oil finish without any stain at all.

655kg/m³ dry
Lowseasonal movement
Oilfinish we use

Sheesham

Dalbergia sissoo

Dark chocolate streaks running through a golden brown. It is heavy and blunts a chisel quickly, so we cut joints in it first thing in the morning when the edges are fresh. Best on pieces that stay put — a bed, an almirah, a sideboard.

770kg/m³ dry
Highhardness
Waxfinish we use

Mango

Mangifera indica

Cut from orchards once the trees stop fruiting, so nothing is felled for it. Pale, soft and quick to work, with occasional grey spalting we leave visible. We use it for drawer sides, carcass interiors and anything painted.

530kg/m³ dry
Orchardsourced
Shellacfinish we use

Babul

Vachellia nilotica

Grown on field edges within an hour of the shop. Hard, interlocked and stubborn under hand tools, but almost nothing wears it out. We keep it for chair legs, tool handles and the stretchers that take the most abuse.

835kg/m³ dry
<60 kmfrom the shop
Oilfinish we use

From log to living room

About twelve weeks for a large piece. You are welcome at any point along the way.

  1. Step 1

    Measure the room

    We come to you, measure the space and the doorways, and look at what the light does through the day.

  2. Step 2

    Draw and price

    A full-size drawing of the piece plus a fixed quote. Nothing is ordered until you have signed off on both.

  3. Step 3

    Choose the boards

    You pick the actual planks off the rack. Grain, colour and the knots you want kept get decided here.

  4. Step 4

    Cut the joints

    Six to ten weeks at the bench. We send photographs every Friday so you can watch it come together.

  5. Step 5

    Deliver and fit

    We carry it in, level it, and come back after one monsoon to check the joints and re-oil the surface.
